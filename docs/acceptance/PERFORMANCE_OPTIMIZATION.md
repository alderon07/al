# Performance optimization acceptance criteria

- PO-001: Compute structural-import source identity once per invocation. Preserve origin hashes, rendered bytes, diagnostics, ranges, bounds, and no-execution behavior for Bash and Zsh. At 10,000 synthetic entries, reduce median structural import time by at least 80% against the recorded Linux baseline.
- PO-002: Diagnose and remove the measured Zsh catalog startup bottleneck. Use genuine Bash and Zsh PTYs at 100, 1,000, and 10,000 synthetic entries. Preserve complete membership, readonly rejection, alias replacement behavior, verified-byte loading, and no entry execution during startup. Reduce the 10,000-entry Zsh startup median by at least 70% against the recorded Linux baseline.
- PO-003: Reduce installed catalog entry-loading allocations by at least 20% at 10,000 entries through measured redundant work. Preserve strict JSON rejection, canonical bytes, exact input hashes, generation identity, approvals, executable membership, native drift checks, and pending/installed entry states. Do not retain cross-call caches that hide filesystem changes.
- PO-004: Reduce typo and no-match search allocations by at least 25% at 10,000 entries. Preserve every match, stable ranking, normalization, context scoring, closest-entry behavior, and independence across concurrent calls.
- PO-005: Record repeated before/after measurements under comparable synthetic conditions, including allocation counts. Run focused behavior tests, actual Bash/Zsh PTYs, and the required `make fmt check` gate before reporting success. Use disposable homes and no private user data. Record platform and measurement limits.
